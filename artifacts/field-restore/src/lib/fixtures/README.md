# Outdoor wind regression fixture

## Provenance and permission

`wind-night-stereo.wav` is a two-second excerpt of an outdoor recording supplied
by its recordist, Lars Lentz. His description was: “Night recording with wind and
passing car.” His permission was: “Free for any use.” This permission covers the
fixture's use and redistribution here; it is not presented as a named Creative
Commons license. No location, equipment, or recording date is asserted.

Original upload: `0_261003_0040_excerpt_1791885104230.wav`, stereo 44,100 Hz,
32-bit IEEE float, 274.973061 seconds. Original SHA-256:
`8404fdc0614ff40705ac45578e6f06d986a9fee5a507b3d01b30b1c36e60f067`.
The approximately 97 MB upload is excluded from Git and deployment.

## Compact derivation

The fixture contains source seconds **35.000–37.000**, keeping both recorded
channels in their original order and at their original gain. This is a sustained
gust-dominated passage, with unequal levels between the microphones. It is not
claimed to contain an animal call or a passing car within these two seconds.

The only conversions are resampling to 24,000 Hz and quantizing to signed 16-bit
PCM (no normalization, denoising, mixing, or channel duplication). Duration:
2 seconds; channels: 2; frames: 48,000; size: 192,078 bytes. SHA-256:
`9d5a5c086c01054015a90ba6c856ba53a9cc0c00f226f817a09e2c3ecfe8a364`.

Recreate with FFmpeg (the original upload is needed; tests do not need FFmpeg):

```sh
ffmpeg -ss 35 -i 0_261003_0040_excerpt_1791885104230.wav \
  -t 2 -map 0:a:0 -ar 24000 -c:a pcm_s16le -map_metadata -1 \
  wind-night-stereo.wav
```

## What the test establishes — and what it does not

The user approved **hybrid** coverage because no real low-call recording was
available. `../wind-outdoor.test.mjs` adds reproducible 105–135 Hz gliding calls
with second and third harmonics, before, during, and well after the recorded
gust. The recording starts one second into the six-second test passage, with a
10 ms taper at each edge to avoid abrupt splice edges. These calls and the
wind-free intervals are synthetic, not recordings of real animal calls.

The tests compare 140, 300, and 700 Hz crossovers against a DC-corrected reference,
in memory and via bounded WAV reads at two non-window-aligned chunk sizes.
Background-only subtraction isolates the gust-overlap call response, **only
after asserting identical detector history through that probe**. Clean calls are
measured directly in the delivered output, without subtraction. The natural stereo
excerpt exercises unequal microphone levels. A separately labelled right-mic-only
variant routes the recorded left channel to the right test channel and leaves
the left call wind-free; it checks linked reduction and channel-swap symmetry.

In this one-mic mixture the call briefly produces an idle detector decision at
test second 2.0, and the gust is detected again at 2.1. The tests retain that
observation rather than assuming perfect wind classification. The overlap
measurement ends at 1.98, before those decisions differ from the background-only
render. Whole-passage memory/file parity includes the idle blip and both fades.

This covers real gust texture interacting with known wanted signals. It does
**not** establish real animal-call classification, natural call/gust overlap,
stereo spatial fidelity for a natural call, or microphone overload recovery.
The excerpt has headroom and is not an overload fixture. Do not describe these
tests as confirmation that arbitrary real low calls are protected.

## Controlled digitally clipped derivatives (not microphone overload)

`controlled-clipped-wind.mjs` derives two small deterministic test passages
**in memory at test time** from the permission-cleared compact fixture above.
No additional recording or binary asset is needed. The same “Free for any use”
permission applies to this derivation. Reproduce with:

```sh
pnpm --filter @workspace/field-restore exec tsx --test src/lib/wind-outdoor.test.mjs
```

Each passage has 144,000 frames (six seconds), two channels, at 24,000 Hz.
The two-second recording starts at test second 1; all other samples are zero.
For each recorded frame `i`, preserve microphone order and compute:

```text
taper = min(1, i / 240, (48000 - 1 - i) / 240)
output[24000 + i] = Float32(max(-1, min(1, recorded[i] * taper * gain)))
```

`gain` is either **8** or **24** (approximately +18.06 or +27.60 dB).
Gain is applied before hard digital clipping at ±1; the 10 ms edge taper is also
applied before clipping. There is no added call, normalization, denoising,
resampling, randomness, or channel duplication. Unlike the unclipped hybrid
test, these passages deliberately lose information at the rails.

The runtime WAV is 32-bit IEEE float stereo, 1,152,044 bytes, generated by
`encodeWav` without another clipping or quantization stage. Reproducible SHA-256:

| Gain | WAV SHA-256 | Left / right samples exactly at ±1 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 8× | `607ca3a398538802df688a5a5bb6b48378db1d28af171bba27bcfd696d3c87f6` | 8,419 / 838 |
| 24× | `570764dffd067e85a026f0f542c529f4372ac704acdf830f6f00db5ab6cc2332` | 31,859 / 19,434 |

These counts are within each channel's 48,000-frame recorded interval. Both
channels reach both rails and repeatedly enter and leave saturation. The
moderate right-channel clipping occurs only within the gust; the severe variant
extends closer to the tapered onset and release.

### Stability, history, and discontinuity checks

Both derivatives exercise automatic Medium detection, 72% reduction, smoothing
50, and **40, 140, 300, 700, and 1,600 Hz** crossovers. These tests explicitly run
the mandatory automatic DC stage before Wind Removal. They require engagement
during the gust and an idle transition after it, without assuming perfect
classification while clipping is present. Complete detector histories must
match across crossovers and across memory and sequential bounded WAV reads
of **257** and **4,093** frames. WAV output matches memory sample-for-sample;
the complete final streaming snapshot also matches a one-shot render, including
filter history, detector accumulators, and both fade envelopes. Input arrays,
decoded read chunks, original fixture samples, and WAV bytes stay unchanged.

Tests report maximum absolute adjacent-sample differences separately for:

- the raw clipped source (`sourceJump`);
- its DC-only reference (`dcJump`);
- DC plus Wind output (`outputJump`);
- Wind's added correction: `max(abs(Δoutput - ΔDC-only))` (`correctionJump`);
- enlargement of existing jumps:
  `max(0, abs(Δoutput) - abs(ΔDC-only))` (`excessJump`).

Subtracting aligned differences before measuring their maximum avoids blaming
Wind Removal for source clipping edges or the mandatory DC stage. A small
measurement regression test injects an edge below an existing source maximum
to confirm that a global output maximum alone would miss the added defect.
These metrics are computed over the whole passage and separately at gust onset
(1.0–1.2 s), release (2.8–4.0 s), every rail entry/exit (±2 samples), every
detector event (±2 ms), and both WAV read schedules' seams (±2 samples).

Baseline whole-passage correction differences and fixed regression ceilings
(normalized amplitude units, **not audibility thresholds**):

| Crossover | 8× measured / ceiling | 24× measured / ceiling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 40 Hz | 0.00788 / 0.010 | 0.01411 / 0.018 |
| 140 Hz | 0.01883 / 0.025 | 0.04146 / 0.055 |
| 300 Hz | 0.03862 / 0.050 | 0.08674 / 0.110 |
| 700 Hz | 0.08966 / 0.115 | 0.19953 / 0.250 |
| 1,600 Hz | 0.21338 / 0.270 | 0.46939 / 0.560 |

The same ceilings bound excess jumps. Higher crossovers remove faster-varying
content from the clipped waveform, so a single tight ceiling across settings
would mistake expected filter response for a new click. Detector-event probes
have tighter ceilings: 0.004 / 0.008 / 0.016 / 0.035 / 0.080 for the 8× variant
in ascending crossover order, and 0.003 for all 24× settings.

Raw source maximum jumps are approximately 0.61865 (8×) and 1.70874 (24×).
All output is finite, with peak ceilings of 0.9 and 1.4 respectively; measured
peaks are approximately 0.79040 and at most 1.25215. **Bounded is not the same
as export headroom**: the severe variant can exceed ±1 after filtering. No
limiter is added to hide this. The final second must settle below 0.0001 after
detector and envelope release.

### Limits

This is **staged digital clipping of recorded gust texture**, not observed
microphone overload. It cannot reproduce capsule nonlinearity, preamp recovery,
AGC, asymmetric analog saturation, or equipment-specific distortion. It checks
deterministic stability and regression limits, not perceptual freedom from
harshness, real overload classification, call preservation under overload, or
reconstruction of samples lost to saturation. A reduction in a jump metric is
not evidence that lost audio was recovered. The existing unclipped hybrid checks
remain separate and retain their original scope.
